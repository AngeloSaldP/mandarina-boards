Route 1

Pre-rendered frames, no WebGL

The home keeps spec §5.1: no WebGL. Scrolling scrubs 96 Blender frames in which the lamp warms from dim and the camera settles to the hero framing (height 4.2, vertical field of view 30°). The first frame is an image with fixed dimensions; the rest load after the page's load event. Under reduced motion only the final still is shown.

The seal break

Rim and M fade over 0 to 220 ms; the 10 segments slide apart along their lines over 0 to 720 ms, fading from 380 ms; an accent flash to 600 ms; 20 embers rise; the lamp warms by 25% over 1200 ms. 37 frames, in the overhead framing and close up.

Decision: route 1, pre-rendered frames

Bytes
  • First paint: frame 1 as an image, 6.0 KB (desktop and phone).
  • Approach, all 96 frames (WebP q80): 1,275 KB at 1600x1000, 1,564 KB at 900x1200; they load after load.
  • Seal break, 37 frames: 843 KB overhead, 1,703 KB close up.
  • JS 3.9 KB and CSS 2.5 KB gzip (demo page, fonts aside).
Frame time
Scrubbing for 10 s: p50 6.9 ms, p95 7.0 ms, no frame over 16.7 ms (headed Chromium, Intel Iris Xe (D3D11), 1440x900, a 144 Hz display). The display caps it at 144 Hz, so this shows it keeps up, not its true cost (one drawImage per frame).
What it needs
Blender renders per framing (about 3 min of GPU for 96 frames), a frame CDN (R2), about 3 KB of scroll code, and nothing at runtime beyond 2D canvas. Reduced motion shows the final still. Any change to the scene means re-rendering.
Spec lines it changes
  • §5.1: the hero still becomes a scrubbed sequence (still no WebGL, so that line holds).
  • §10.1 home images 250 KB: the first frame (6 KB) and the final still (21 to 26 KB) fit, but the full sequence is 5 to 6 times the budget. Either the frames after load sit outside the budget, or the sequence drops to about 24 frames.
  • §7.4: the seal break becomes frames (843 KB per framing) instead of code.
  • §3.5: the faint kicker and bounce (lookdev-06) are a deviation from "one lamp from above".
