S1

Decision: the sealed book hero

The move
A viewport-scale headline split around the closed book, interleaved in depth (line one behind, line two in front), a mono detail layer with one live value (studio time), and a filmic lamp glow with static grain. The preview button plays the spec §7.4 seal break: segments part, the counter runs 10 to 0, the lamp warms.
Where it comes from
Editorial magazine covers, where the masthead runs behind the cover subject and a second line runs over it; the instrument readouts on studio portfolio heroes; and film photography, where bright areas compress softly instead of clipping and grain holds the gradients together.
Cost
3.0 KB JS / 2.9 KB CSS gzip, against the home page budget (60 KB JS, 20 KB CSS). Images: 0 KB shipped; the lamp glow and grain are generated in the browser (about 15 ms once per session, cached in sessionStorage).
Reduced motion
No scroll parallax and no counter roll. The seal break becomes a 240 ms fade with a 200 ms accent flash and no embers; the lamp warms with a 400 ms opacity change. The clock still updates its digits, without movement.
Spec change
§5.1 (hero): the H1 moves from centred under the book to split around it, and the HUD line is added. §3.3: the hero H1 grows beyond the 4.5rem clamp, to viewport scale. §7.4: the lamp is a generated texture instead of the CSS conic beam. The studio time zone (America/Lima) needs confirming.

S2

Decision: the seal under a loupe

The move
The wax seal drawn large, in relief (10 segments, mandarin to wine wax, a raised rim and an embossed M), with a loupe that magnifies a sharper copy three times. Idle, the loupe drifts on a slow scripted path; a pointer takes it over; touch drags it. A ring of mono text turns once every two minutes, with a mandarin segment between words.
Where it comes from
A jeweller's loupe over a hallmark, and banknote micro-printing that only appears under magnification: the detail is the proof of authenticity. The turning ring comes from the legends around coins and notarial seals.
Cost
3.3 KB JS / 1.5 KB CSS gzip, against the home page budget (60 KB JS, 20 KB CSS). Images: 0 KB; the seal, the wax texture and the lens are SVG in the page.
Reduced motion
The loupe rests over the rim and the micro-engraving and does not drift or ease; it moves only when you drag it, tap the wax or press an arrow key, and then it jumps. The ring does not turn.
Spec change
§5.1 gains a seal section between the hero and the pillars. §3.4 gains the large relief seal and the micro-engraving as a brand detail. §7.3: the loupe is this viewport's reactive effect and the ring its ambient one, so the ember glow must not reach this section.

S3

Decision: sealed projects as glyph silhouettes that decode open

The move
Every sealed project is shown as a mandarin glyph grid of its poster, generated at build time. Unsealing reads the grid outward from the seal, then breaks the card along the seal's ten segment lines into the real poster. Headings resolve from glyphs once, under 900 ms, as an overlay over the real text.
Where it comes from
The before and after decode, and the brightness-to-glyph mapping, seen on an ASCII-art tool's website; the seal break from our own spec (section 7.4). Rewritten from the ideas: our posters, our ramp, our script.
Cost
4.4 KB JS and 1.9 KB CSS gzip (unminified), plus 0.4 KB gzip per glyph grid inlined in the HTML. On the home page that is about 7% of the 60 KB JS budget and 10% of the 20 KB CSS budget; inside the catalog it fits the 39 KB of headroom left by the book. No images ship for a sealed project: the grid is text, so no CLS and no LCP cost. The placeholder poster here is 1.3 KB gzip.
Reduced motion
No glyph scramble: the heading is shown at once. Unsealing becomes a 240 ms crossfade from the glyphs to the poster with a 200 ms accent flash; sealing again is a 160 ms fade.
Spec change
Section 6.1 adds a build-time silhouette (glyph grid) to each public project entry; section 8.4 states that only the silhouette ships publicly and the poster arrives with the session (on this board the placeholder poster is public, as a demo). Section 7.4 adds the glyph read before the seal break, and the glyph heading as an alternative to the line-mask headline.

S4

Decision: the steps as a pinned counter

The move
"Cómo trabajamos" pins for four screens of scroll. The scroll position is the state: an odometer counter ticks 01 to 04, one leaf of a CSS 3D placeholder book turns per step (scrubbed by the scroll, so it follows the finger and settles at rest points), and the diagram on the page that lands draws itself. "Siguiente paso" scrolls to the next rest point and sets the same state at once.
Where it comes from
Studio process pages that pin a section and let scroll play a sequence, joined with the old print idea of a numbered plate: one figure per page, a folio in the corner. The odometer is the mechanical counter of a press run, rolling rather than swapping digits.
Cost
JS 1.8 KB / CSS 3.0 KB gzip, plus 2.5 KB of markup and SVG in the page. Draws from the home budget (60 KB JS, 20 KB CSS); no images, no WebGL. In browsers with scroll-driven animations the scrub costs no JS per frame; elsewhere a passive scroll handler writes one custom property per frame while the section is on screen.
Reduced motion
No page turn: the spread crossfades over 200 ms. The diagram is drawn already, the counter changes instantly, the step text swaps with a 160 ms fade. Scrolling and the button still advance the steps.
Spec change
§5.1 item 4 ("How we work") becomes a pinned section with the book as its object; §7.4 gains the step counter as a signature moment. Fraunces in our subset has no tabular figures, so the counter sets each digit in a fixed-width cell; §3.3 would keep the subset as it is.

S5

Decision: the catalog as a typeset index with a live preview

The move
The DOM list from spec section 6.2 becomes a typeset index: number, title, type, year and outcome per row. Hover or focus swaps a single preview (decoded before it is shown, so it never flashes) and gives the row the accent; sealed rows preview their glyph silhouette only. On touch the first tap selects and the second opens; Enter opens from the keyboard.
Where it comes from
The index-with-preview pattern of studio portfolio sites, and the "decode, then swap, drop stale requests" image swap seen on an ASCII-art tool's site; rewritten. The sealed silhouettes are S3's build-time grids.
Cost
2.0 KB JS and 2.1 KB CSS gzip (unminified), plus the two sealed glyph grids inlined (0.4 and 0.3 KB gzip) and three placeholder posters (2.0 KB gzip together). Against the catalog phone deck budget (45 KB JS, 20 KB CSS) that is under 5% and 11%. Real posters would be the 16:10 media of spec section 14, fetched on first hover or focus.
Reduced motion
The preview swaps with a 160 ms opacity fade and no scale; sealed silhouettes appear without the glyph scramble; the active row changes colour only.
Spec change
Section 6.2: the always-present DOM list is designed as this index (also the reduced-motion "list view" of section 7.6), with the "La próxima página" row last. Section 7.5 adds a row: index row, hover or focus swaps the preview; touch, first tap selects. Section 6.1 needs the silhouette field from S3.

S6

Decision: showcases as live interface

The move
An open showcase spread shows the tool itself, built in HTML rather than as a screenshot: its figures tick to their values in tabular figures, its chart draws, its notifications stack in, once per entry. The real values sit in the accessible text from the first paint, so nothing is announced twice and nothing waits on the animation.
Where it comes from
Product launch pages that show software as a living interface instead of a still, and the counting odometers of departure boards: values that arrive rather than appear. Here it stays restrained: one pass, no loop, a replay button for those who want it.
Cost
JS 1.9 KB / CSS 2.1 KB gzip, plus 2.7 KB of markup, no images (the screen is markup, so it is sharp at any size and costs less than its 16:10 poster). It would draw from the catalog budget (170 KB JS, 25 KB CSS) or the home (60 KB, 20 KB) if a showcase card used it.
Reduced motion
The final values, the drawn chart and the full notification stack show at once; "Reproducir" refreshes them with a 160 ms fade.
Spec change
§14 (media contract) gains a third kind of showcase media: a live HTML screen with placeholder or anonymised data, beside posters and video. §6.3 would render it as a DOM overlay on the open spread, not as a texture.

S7

Decision: the request lives in the envelope

The move
"Pide acceso" stops being a form below the fold: the field sits on the sealed envelope from the desk (§3.5). A valid email or code melts the seal in 14 vertical slices that sag and drip under an accent flash, the flap lifts and a letter rises with "Solicitud recibida". The real text is a normal form, a role="alert" error and a role="status" message.
Where it comes from
Sealing wax itself: a seal that is softened rather than broken, and the ceremony of a letter that answers. The slice technique is the old film trick of cutting an image into strips and letting each fall on its own timing, done here with clip-paths and transforms only.
Cost
JS 4.9 KB / CSS 2.6 KB gzip plus 3.3 KB of markup, including the WebGL variant (about 2.7 KB of the JS when gzipped alone, loaded with the page but inert until switched on). Draws from the home budget (60 KB JS, 20 KB CSS). The CSS version uses no images; the WebGL one draws its texture from the same seal with Canvas 2D.
Reduced motion
Per §7.6: the seal fades over 240 ms under a 200 ms accent flash, with no melt and no particles; the flap and the letter swap with a 200 ms crossfade instead of moving. The WebGL switch has no effect.
Spec change
§5.1 gains the envelope as the home's request entry, and §7.4 gains the melt beside the seal break. The WebGL variant would need §5.1's "no WebGL on the home page" relaxed to "only on demand"; the CSS version needs no change there.

S9

Decision: adopt the details kit and the corner-bracket seal

The move
Fifteen controls for the forms, the catalog and the home, built once in our tokens; and concept E: on hover (fine pointer) and keyboard focus, four corner brackets draw in around a card or button and its border closes into an accent hairline, a small "seal" around what you are about to open. The 2 px accent ring always stays.
Where it comes from
Fifteen MIT-licensed ideas from uiverse-io/galaxy picked in design research 2 (§2), each credited above with its path; we kept only the idea and wrote the markup, styles and motion from scratch, so no notice ships. Concept E comes from research §3 E and idea 04.
Cost
The whole kit: 1.3 KB JS and 5.0 KB CSS gzip (1,377 and 5,153 bytes), plus a 252-byte grain tile. Concept E alone: 0.8 KB CSS (843 bytes), 0 KB JS. On the home that is 2% of the 60 KB JS budget and 25% of the 20 KB CSS budget for the full kit; a page only ships the pieces it uses.
Reduced motion
Nothing travels: the brackets, the check, the radio dot, the segment highlight, the switch, the floating label and the tooltip change in a 160 ms opacity or colour step; cards do not lift; the loader segment stands still at the centre of its hairline and only the status text changes.
Spec change
§7.5 gains rows for the corner brackets (cards and buttons: draw in 220 ms; reduced: appear; touch: on focus), the tooltip and the loader; §9.1 notes that the brackets are decorative and never replace the ring; §7.3 lists the brackets beside the comet.

H The hero routes

Three ways to make the book on the desk

No demo here: a choice. The hero is the book under the lamp, seen first on the home and opened on the catalog. It can be drawn live, played back from frames rendered in advance, or both. Pick a route to highlight it; the figures are measured or taken from the spec budgets (§10.1).

Route none chosen

01 Chosen

Real-time 3D

131 KB JS gzip, measured

What it costs
The book spike's three.js chunk is 134,400 bytes gzip (131 KB). On the catalog it fits the 150 KB island with 19 KB to spare (39 KB of the page's 170 KB). On the home it does not fit: it is 2.2 times the 60 KB JS budget on its own, against about 4.7 KB today, and the spec allows no WebGL there (§5.1). Add up to 1.5 MB of KTX2 textures, lazy, and GPU memory under 64 MB (§10.2).
What it needs
The device gate and 1.5 s test render of §6.2, tier 2 and the deck and list as fallbacks, context-loss rebuilds, a poster as the LCP element while the canvas loads, and the owner's phone run of the benchmark passing (50 fps or more, p95 frame 20 ms or less) before anything is built on it.
Risks
Every number so far comes from software WebGL, which is not representative; the phone run is pending. INP and battery on mid-range phones, and a slow first frame on Slow 4G.

Open the book spike Run the 30 s benchmark

02 Chosen

Pre-rendered sequence

1-2 KB JS, estimated

What it costs
Almost no script: a small player that steps frames or drives a short clip. The weight moves to media: the home allows 250 KB of images and 0 of video, so either about eight AVIF frames of 20 to 25 KB each (a stepped, not fluid, opening) or a clip of 600 KB or less (§14), which needs a home video budget the spec does not have.
What it needs
The 3D scene rendered offline frame by frame (by our own script from the spike's scene, or a renderer), AVIF and WebM encodes, the first frame as the LCP poster, the last frame as the reduced-motion and no-JS state, and a re-render whenever the book's look or the project list changes.
Risks
It cannot be touched: no drag, no page turns, so the catalog still needs another book. It reads as video, it costs bytes on Slow 4G, and the frames go stale as the catalog grows.

03 Chosen

Hybrid

4.7 KB JS on the home

What it costs
The home keeps today's script and shows one pre-rendered still of the desk, inside the 250 KB image budget, as §5.1 already says. The catalog loads the 131 KB live book only when it comes into view and the device passes the gate, inside its 150 KB island.
What it needs
Stills rendered from the same scene and the same camera as the live book, one per tier, so the poster and the canvas match; a 240 ms crossfade from poster to canvas after the first rendered frame; the fallback chain tier 1, tier 2, deck, list; and the phone run to decide whether the live part is three.js or the CSS book (plan 1).
Risks
A visible seam if the still and the live render drift apart in light or angle; two render paths to keep in step; the crossfade must not count as a layout shift.
H

Decision: how the hero is made

The move
Choose one of the three routes. The board leans to the hybrid: it is the only route inside every budget today, it is what §5.1 and §6.3 already describe, and it lets the phone run decide the live part without touching the home.
Where it comes from
Spec §5.1 (the home's still), §6.2 and §6.3 (the book and its tiers), §10.1 and §10.2 (budgets and three.js), §14 (media); the book spike and its plan (pass: under 200 KB gzip and 50 fps or more on a mid-range phone).
Cost
This chapter: 363 bytes JS and 724 bytes CSS gzip, board only; it ships nothing to the site. The routes' own costs are in each card above, against §10.1.
Reduced motion
This chapter does not move: choosing a route changes a border colour in 160 ms. Per route: real-time opens in list view with a poster (§7.6); the sequence shows its last frame; the hybrid shows the still and crossfades spreads over 200 ms.
Spec change
Real-time 3D: §5.1 (WebGL on the home), §10.1 (home JS from 60 KB to about 140 KB), §7.3. Pre-rendered sequence: §5.1, §10.1 and §14 (a home video budget, or a frame budget), and §6.3 if it also replaces the live book. Hybrid: none, except a line in §6.3 on the poster-to-canvas handoff.
